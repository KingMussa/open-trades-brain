# Rigging Fundamentals

*ASME B30.9 / B30.26 principles. Know the load, know the gear, know the plan — or don't lift.*

## Sling Angle = Real Tension (the math that hurts people)

Sling angles measured **from horizontal**. Tension multiplier per leg:

| Angle from horizontal | Tension multiplier |
|---|---|
| 90° (vertical) | 1.00 |
| 60° | 1.155 |
| 45° | **1.414** |
| 30° | **2.00** |

- Each leg tension = (load ÷ legs) × multiplier. **2-leg bridle at 30° on a 4,000 lb load = 4,000 lb PER LEG** — same as the whole load, per leg
- Never rig below 30°; 45° minimum preferred; 60° is the happy zone
- Equal legs ≠ equal tension unless the load is balanced — find the CG

## Hitch Types (capacity vs vertical rating)

- **Vertical:** 100% of WLL
- **Choker:** ~**75–80%** of vertical (choke angle <120° reduces more); never choke on the splice/fitting
- **Basket:** ~**200%** (both legs vertical, balanced); basket with legs at angle = apply sling-angle math
- **2-leg bridle:** each leg carries per the angle table above

## Hardware Rules

- **Shackles:** screw pin for temporary; bolt-type for long-term/vibration. Load the pin/bow centerline — **never side-load a shackle beyond 45°**; never replace the pin with a bolt
- **Eye bolts:** vertical lift only at full rating; **any angle destroys them** (use swivel hoist rings for angular lifts); shoulder seated flush
- **Hooks:** latch closed, load in the BOWL (never the tip/point); no side loading
- Taglines on anything that can spin or swing — hands off the load itself

## Slings

- **Wire rope:** field rule vertical WLL ≈ **(diameter in inches)² × 8 tons** (6×19/6×37 IWRC). Reject for: 10% broken wires in one lay, kinks, crushing, birdcaging, heat damage, core protrusion
- **Grade 80 chain** (published single-leg WLL): 9/32" = 3,500 lb · 3/8" = 7,100 lb · 1/2" = 12,000 lb · 5/8" = 18,100 lb · 3/4" = 28,300 lb. The only chain grade for overhead lifting; shorten with proper clutch, never knots/bolts
- **Synthetic web:** cut risk on edges — softeners mandatory on anything sharp; reject on cuts, burns, broken stitching, worn red core yarn
- **Roundslings (EN color code):** violet 1t · green 2t · yellow 3t · grey 4t · red 5t · brown 6t · blue 8t · orange 10t+. The label is the rating — no label, no lift
- ALL slings need a legible tag (WLL by hitch type). **No tag = out of service**

## Weight Math (get the load right first)

- Steel: 490 lb/ft³ · Pipe steel lb/ft = 10.69 × (OD − wall) × wall
- Full pipe = steel + water (0.3405 × ID² lb/ft) — a 10" Sch 40 full of water ≈ 115 lb/ft
- Water: 8.34 lb/gal · Concrete: ~150 lb/ft³ (4,000 lb/yd³) · Wood: ~35–50 lb/ft³
- Plate weight: 1" steel plate = 40.8 lb/ft²
- When in doubt: verified weight from drawings/submittal, or weigh it — NEVER guess on a pick

## Lift Planning Levels

1. **Ordinary:** standard load, clear path — plan it verbally at the huddle
2. **Critical (needs written plan):** >75% crane capacity, multi-crane, over live equipment/personnel areas, blind picks, near power lines, odd CG
3. Written plan covers: verified weight + CG, crane configuration + capacity chart page, rigging list with WLLs, radius boom length counterweight, ground conditions/outrigger mats, path + set-down zone, exclusion zone, signal person, wind limits (typically stop ~20–25 mph or per chart)

## Ground & Crane Basics

- Know the ground: soil bearing, voids (vaults/basements), recent rain. Outriggers FULLY extended on mats, floats off the ground
- Capacity drops fast with radius — check the chart at the MAXIMUM radius of the pick, not at set-down
- Side loading a boom = folded boom. Hoist straight over the load
- Power lines: absolute minimum 10 ft up to 50 kV (+0.4" per kV over 50) — more is better, spotter assigned
