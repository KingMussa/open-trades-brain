# Pipe, Fittings & Valves — Identification and Use

## Pipe & Tube

| Material | Identified by | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon steel Sch 40/80 | Black or galv, stenciled A53/A106 | CHW/HW/CW mains, steam, gas |
| Stainless 304/316 Sch 10/40 | Bright, non-magnetic-ish (304 slightly) | RO/DI, process, corrosive |
| Copper Type K/L/M | K=green stamp, L=blue, M=red | K: underground/med gas; L: hydronic/domestic; M: light domestic (check spec — many jobs prohibit M) |
| ACR copper | Cleaned/capped, dehydrated | Refrigerant only — never substitute plumbing copper |
| CPVC/PVC | Cream (CPVC) / white (PVC) | Condensate, some CW/PCW, DWV |
| PEX | Flexible coils, red/blue/white | Domestic water distribution |

- Sch 40 vs Sch 80: **same OD**, 80 has thicker wall = smaller ID = higher pressure rating. OD never changes — fittings fit either
- "Nominal" size ≠ any real dimension above 1/8". Memorize ODs: 1/2"=0.840, 3/4"=1.050, 1"=1.315, 1-1/2"=1.900, 2"=2.375, 3"=3.500, 4"=4.500, 6"=6.625, 8"=8.625, 10"=10.750, 12"=12.750
- Copper tube is sized by NOMINAL — actual OD = nominal + 1/8" (1/2" tube = 5/8" OD)

## Thread Identification

- **NPT**: tapered, seals on threads + dope/tape. Tape clockwise (so threading on doesn't unwrap it), 2–3 wraps, off the first thread
- **NPS**: straight — needs a gasket/washer or mechanical joint, will NOT seal by itself
- **Flare (45° SAE)**: refrigeration/gas — metal-to-metal cone seat, no dope ever on the seat
- **Compression**: ferrule bites tube; one-time use ferrule

## Valve Types — What Each Is FOR

| Valve | Use it for | Never use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Gate | Full open/closed isolation, low ΔP straight-through | **Throttling** — it wire-draws and destroys the seat |
| Globe | Throttling/flow regulation | Where pressure drop matters |
| Ball | Quick quarter-turn isolation | Fine throttling |
| Butterfly | Large-line isolation, some throttling | Bubble-tight shutoff criticals (unless rated) — note lug vs wafer; dead-end service needs lug |
| Check (swing) | Horizontal lines, low velocity reversal | Pulsating flow, vertical-down |
| Check (spring/silent) | Pump discharge, vertical-up, water-hammer-prone | Dirty service without strainer |
| Balancing (circuit setter) | Setting design GPM with PT ports | Isolation (not bubble-tight) — pair with a real isolation valve |
| AFCV / PICV | Automatic flow limiting / pressure-independent control | Field adjustment (factory-set cartridge) |
| PRV | Pressure reduction (water, steam, air) | Dead-end without relief consideration |
| Solenoid | Remote on/off control | Manual bypass absence on critical service |

- Install check and globe valves with **flow arrow matching flow** — a backwards check valve stops the system dead
- Rising-stem vs non-rising: rising tells you position at a glance; non-rising saves headroom
- Test ports/PT plugs on the SYSTEM side of balancing valves; readable orientation (commissioning walks check this)

## Strainers & Specialties

- Y-strainer: screen leg DOWNSTREAM and down (water) or horizontal (steam/gas); blow-down valve on the cap for service
- Suction diffuser at pump inlets; triple-duty valve = check + balance + shutoff in one body
- Air separator at the system's hottest/ lowest-pressure point (usually near pump suction + expansion tank connection — "point of no pressure change")
- Expansion tank connection = point of no pressure change; pump adds head DOWNSTREAM of it
- Dielectric union/flange REQUIRED at every copper↔steel transition (electrolysis eats the steel)
