# Foreman Playbook — Running Your Crew

*The daily/weekly operating system. Print this in your head.*

## The Day (in order)

1. **Before the crew (15–30 min early):** walk your work areas. Know today's plan, material status, and blockers BEFORE the huddle. Check: lifts inspected/charged, tools staged, drawings current revision
2. **Morning huddle (5–10 min, every day, no exceptions):**
   - Safety topic + today's specific hazards (overhead work, energized, hot work, lifts)
   - Each man's assignment: WHO, WHAT, WHERE, by WHEN
   - Material/tool needs for today AND tomorrow
   - One question that always surfaces problems: "What's in your way?"
3. **Mid-morning walk:** verify production started clean — right material, right elevation, right detail. Fixing at 9 AM is free; fixing at 3 PM costs a day
4. **After lunch walk:** production check against the day's goal. Adjust crew splits if an area is behind
5. **End of day (last 20 min):** area left SAFE and clean (broom-clean standard), open pipe ends capped, tools counted, lifts parked/charging. Tomorrow's first-hour work staged
6. **Paperwork daily (non-negotiable, 15 min):** daily report, time coding, quantities installed, photos of covered work BEFORE it's covered

## The Daily Report — Write It Like a Lawyer Reads It

- Date, weather, temp (matters for concrete/pipe/claims)
- Manpower by name + trade + hours, and WHO they worked for if mixed crews
- Work performed by AREA and GRID LINE, not "worked on piping" — "installed 60 LF 4\" SCHWS grid 7.2–7.3, elev +14'"
- Deliveries received + condition; visitors (inspectors, GC, owner, QA)
- Delays/disruptions: what, when, WHO caused it, how long. **If it's not written, it didn't happen** — this is your change-order ammunition
- Safety incidents/near misses, inspections passed/failed

## JHA / Pre-Task Plan (before any new or hazardous task)

1. Task steps in order
2. Hazard at each step (energy, gravity, pinch, fume, weather)
3. Control for each: eliminate → engineer → admin → PPE (in that order)
4. Crew signs it. Review it WHEN conditions change, not just in the morning

## The 2-Week Lookahead (your real job)

Every week, same day, write it:
- Week 1: tasks, manpower needed, material confirmed ON SITE, prereq inspections
- Week 2: tasks, material ORDERED (get PO/tracking), prerequisites (other trades, access, lifts)
- Kill list: every blocker with an OWNER and a DATE. A blocker without a name on it is yours forever

## Production Tracking (simple version that works)

- Know your budget units: e.g., budget = 1.5 man-hr per LF of 4" grooved steel (get rates from PM/estimate)
- Track installed quantities daily; weekly: **hours earned vs hours burned**
- Behind >10%? Don't just add bodies — check the real cause: material, access, rework, waiting on answers, crew mix

## Managing Men

- **Assignment clarity beats motivation.** Men fail from unclear tasks, not laziness
- Pair green with experienced; never two green hands alone on a critical path
- Praise in public, correct in private — correct the ACTION, not the man
- Document performance issues same-day, factual, no adjectives: "Arrived 7:40 vs 7:00 start, 3rd time this month (also 6/12, 6/19)"
- Your best man gets the critical path, not the hardest work — there's a difference
- Toolbox talks: teach ONE thing a week. A crew that learns doesn't leave

## Inspections

- **Self-inspect FIRST** — walk it like the inspector before you call him
- Have ready: approved drawings on site, test records, material certs/submittals, access to the work
- Failed inspection = find root cause same day, fix, re-schedule, and tell the GC before he hears it from the inspector

## Material Discipline

- Nothing fabricated/installed without verifying it's the APPROVED material (submittal, not just "what showed up")
- Receive material properly: count, inspect, note damage, sign "subject to count and inspection"
- Store off ground, protected; copper/stainless separated from carbon steel; gaskets out of sun
- Long-lead items: check status weekly against the lookahead, not when you need them
